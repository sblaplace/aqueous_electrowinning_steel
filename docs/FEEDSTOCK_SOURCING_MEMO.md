# Feedstock Sourcing Memo — The Negative-Cost Iron Beachhead

**Answers RESEARCH_PROGRAM open question Q7:** *"What's the feedstock at 100 kt/yr, and what does it cost per tonne of contained Fe?"*

**Status:** Desktop research, figures only (not a contact list). Compiled 2026-08-02.
**Program thesis:** aqueous electrowinning of iron from a *negative-cost* waste-Fe stream. You are paid to take the feedstock and you sell the iron. The economics work before you ever compete with BOF/DRI.

---

## Executive summary & recommendation

**Recommended single beachhead feedstock: sulfate-route TiO₂-plant copperas (ferrous sulfate heptahydrate, FeSO₄·7H₂O).**

It is the **only** candidate that passes all three quantitative gates a 100 kt/yr beachhead requires *simultaneously*:

| Gate | SPL | **Copperas** | Red mud | AMD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Negative cost confirmed & dollarized | ✅ −$300 to −$600/t Fe | ✅ **−$111 to −$278/t Fe** | ⚠️ −$20 to −$60/t Fe | ❌ net positive |
| Single-site ≥100 kt/yr Fe | ❌ fragmented | ✅ **one plant = 126–250 kt Fe/yr** | ✅ volume, fails next gate | ❌ need all US AMD |
| Minimal processing to EW feed | ✅ already-solution | ✅ **water-soluble, direct feed** | ❌ caustic + refractory leach | ❌ 1,000–100,000× dilution |

- **Copperas** gives a single-site concentrated Fe²⁺ supply at a *negative* cost (the generator pays you), directly soluble into an electrowinning bath — no refractory-oxide leaching, no caustic neutralisation.
- **Spent pickle liquor (SPL)** is the *right near-term stepping stone* (TRL 6–7, direct experimental proof exists) but cannot supply 100 kt/yr from one site: the entire non-regenerated US SPL stream is only ~20–40 kt Fe/yr.
- **Red mud** and **acid-mine drainage** are disqualified with numbers: red mud by ~$15–20M/yr acid cost behind caustic-neutralisation + refractory-leach; AMD by a 1,000–100,000× dilution penalty.

**Candidate site/region (deployment thesis):** a **large sulfate-route TiO₂ refinery in the EU** (e.g. Venator Scarlino/Italy or Duisburg/Germany; Kronos plants) — where disposal pressure is highest (producers *pay* to remove copperas, confirmed by EU Commission M.7061), logistics/regulation are stable, and carbon/Green-Deal incentives support green iron. China's sulfate-route mega-plants (Lomon Billions, ~1 Mt TiO₂/yr) are the alternate supply for scale but carry geopolitical/logistics and re-export friction. The US Rust Belt SPL stream is flagged as the paired near-term co-location site.

**Bottom line for Q7:** at 100 kt/yr, feed off **~556 kt/yr of copperas** drawn from a single large sulfate TiO₂ plant at an effective feedstock cost of **≈ −$150/t Fe (range −$111 to −$278/t Fe)** — i.e. a ~$15M/yr negative feedstock cost (revenue) at full scale.

## 2. Spent pickle liquor (SPL) — the strong near-term stepping stone

**Volumes.** US ~0.7–1.0 Mt SPL/yr (~180–260 M gal/yr); global ~3–5 Mt/yr. ~50–80 L SPL per tonne of steel pickled (HCl). `1 t SPL at 10% Fe = 100 kg contained Fe`.

**Composition.** Spent HCl liquor ~10–12 wt% Fe (as FeCl₂, ~25 wt%), residual HCl 2–5%. Spent H₂SO₄ liquor ~10–12 wt% Fe, residual H₂SO₄ 3–8%. **Directly electrowinnable — no solid phase to leach.**

**Disposal vs regeneration.** Non-regenerated SPL is a genuine liability at **$100–300/t SPL = $1,000–3,000/t contained Fe** (RCRA F019 hazardous-waste hauling, lime neutralisation, landfill). Acid regeneration (spray roasting) costs $30–60/t SPL but only breaks even above ~15–20 m³/hr — so **mini-mills and specialty mills below that threshold treat SPL as pure cost.** ~30–40% of US HCl SPL (≈200–400 kt/yr) is *not* regenerated — the addressable stream.

**Effective cost.** Generator's avoided disposal ≈ processor's revenue → **net −$300 to −$600/t Fe; defensible conservative mid-case −$400/t Fe** (the current internal model's −$416.67 at 12% Fe is consistent).

**Competing pull.** SPL → ferric chloride for water treatment is a real market ($200–400/t solution, i.e. ~$1,200–2,000/t Fe), which *partially* offsets but does not eliminate the negative-cost dynamic (chlorine + NSF certification costs cap it at ~$50–150/t SPL net).

**Scale ceiling (the disqualifier for 100 kt/yr at one site).** 100 kt Fe/yr needs ~1,000 kt SPL/yr. The entire **US non-regenerated stream is only ~200–400 kt/yr → ~20–40 kt Fe/yr.** To reach 100 kt Fe/yr from SPL you must aggregate hundreds of mills and/or pull integrated-mill regeneration feedstock. Fragmented supply, not single-site.

**Proof point.** Badalbayli et al. 2025 (CWRU/LLNL, *ECS Advances*) experimentally electrodeposited >98 wt% pure iron from Cleveland-Cliffs SPL at >85% coulombic efficiency (chloride molten-salt / electrolysis pathway) — **direct experimental validation of the SPL→iron-electrowinning route.**

## 3. Copperas — RECOMMENDED beachhead

**Identity & composition.** FeSO₄·7H₂O = **20.09 wt% Fe (theoretical), ~18 wt% typical commercial.** Water-soluble — hydrate crystals dissolve directly into an Fe(II) sulfate EW bath. No refractory-oxide leach.

**Source.** ~3.5 t copperas per tonne of sulfate-route TiO₂ pigment (ilmenite + H₂SO₄ digestion). Global TiO₂ ~6–7 Mt/yr, ~40–50% sulfate route (>90% in China) → **~20–25 Mt/yr copperas byproduct** (merchant ferrous-sulfate market is only ~0.3 Mt/yr — <2%; the rest is near-zero-value/disposed).

**Single-site scale — the decisive advantage.** One mid/large sulfate plant (200–400 kt TiO₂/yr) generates ~700 kt–1.4 Mt copperas → **~126–250 kt Fe/yr.** A Chinese mega-plant (Lomon Billions ~1 Mt TiO₂/yr) → ~630 kt Fe/yr. **100 kt Fe/yr needs only ~556 kt copperas/yr — comfortably inside a single plant's output.** Supply concentration is the strongest of any candidate.

**Negative cost — confirmed.** EU Commission merger case M.7061: sulfate TiO₂ by-product is "turned into a cost issue" — producers **pay to dispose.** Wet/contaminated copperas takes away at **−$10 to −$50/t → −$111 to −$278/t contained Fe** (at 18% Fe). Even free take-away = $0/t Fe, below the ~$160–190/t Fe of 62% iron-ore fines. Comparison: copperas at any negative-to-$0 price is **the cheapest contained-Fe source in this analysis.**

**Candidate sites.** EU (Venator Scarlino, IT / Duisburg, DE; Kronos Leverkusen — highest disposal pressure, stable regulation, carbon incentives) = **primary.** China (Lomon Billions Jiaozuo/Deyang, CNNC Hua Yuan, Shandong cluster) = scale-first alternate, negative cost likely, but logistics + re-export + sulfate-route-decline risk. India (TTPL, KMML) = smaller (~11 kt Fe/yr per site — insufficient alone).

**Risks.** (1) Sulfate route is declining in the West (chloride route makes no copperas) — secure a long-term offtake or accept a ~10–20 yr feed window from an existing plant. (2) Quality varies (Ti/Cr/V traces) — plan EW-feed purification. (3) 56 wt% water in the hydrate — local co-location avoids freight on water. (4) Competing markets (cement Cr(VI) reduction, coagulant) can erode the negative price over time.

## 4. Red mud — disqualified on processing economics (volume is not the problem)

**Volume is enormous.** ~170 Mt/yr generated globally (142 Mt alumina × ~1.23 t/t), ~4 Gt stockpiled, ~35 Mt Fe/yr theoretical contained. **Volume alone is not the constraint.**

**The problem is extraction.** Iron lives as **refractory hematite + Al-substituted goethite**, "annealed" under Bayer conditions (150–250 °C, NaOH), in a **caustic (pH 10–13), ultrafine (<20 µm)** matrix. Direct acid leach requires: (a) **neutralising residual NaOH first** (0.3–0.8 t acid/t red mud *wasted* on neutralisation), then (b) dissolving the refractory oxides, then (c) purifying Fe from co-dissolved Al/Ti/Na.

**Cost.** Realistic total acid bill for 100 kt Fe/yr ≈ **$15–20M/yr** (the internal model's −$100/t Fe credit *and* $2M/yr acid cost are both optimistic: feedstock credit is more like −$20 to −$60/t Fe, acid cost ~5–10× higher). End-to-end recovered-Fe cost **$200–500/t Fe — comparable to or worse than conventional ore.** Smelting to pig iron ($250–400/t Fe) and reduction-roast + magnetic separation ($150–300/t Fe) recover more mass but are energy/CO₂-heavy, pilot-only, and off-thesis (they are not aqueous electrowinning).

**Role:** a **secondary/experimental** feedstock where co-located with a high-Fe tropical-bauxite refinery in a high-disposal-cost jurisdiction (EU) with cheap acid/power. Not the beachhead.

---

## 5. Acid-mine drainage — disqualified on dilution

**The dilution penalty is decisive.** AMD is 0.001–0.05 wt% Fe — **1,000–100,000× more dilute than iron ore.**

At a typical 100 mg/L Fe, one tonne of Fe needs **10,000 m³ of AMD**; the 100 kt/yr target needs **~1 km³/yr ≈ all US AMD discharge combined.** At 500 mg/L (high-Fe sites) it still needs ~all US AMD; the extreme sites (Iron Mountain, 111 g/L) are rare and low-flow.

**Economics invert.** Treatment costs $0.50–5.00/1,000 gal → **$1.30–13.00 per kg Fe recovered vs $0.10–0.15/kg Fe from ore — 10–100× more expensive.** Iron from AMD is a *waste product to be disposed of*, not a revenue source. (AMD's real value is REE/CM co-recovery — NETL/WVU pilots show 22–115% IRR on REE, with iron as an incidental byproduct.)

**Role:** none for the iron beachhead. It could be a small, grant-funded co-location demo (PA AML set-aside; FY2026 fund $679M) where treatment is already obligatory and iron is a bonus — but it does not answer Q7 at 100 kt/yr.

## 6. Q7 reconciliation — the 100 kt/yr decision in one table

| Candidate | Fe supply @100 kt/yr | Single-site? | Effective Fe cost | Processing cost | **Verdict** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Copperas** | 1 plant → 126–250 kt Fe | ✅ | **−$111 to −$278/t Fe** | Low | **✅ Beachhead** |
| SPL | ~all global SPL | ❌ | −$300 to −$600/t Fe | Minimal | Near-term step, not 100 kt single-site |
| Red mud | volume yes | ✅ | −$20 to −$60/t Fe | **$15–20M/yr acid; $200–500/t Fe** | ❌ Secondary |
| AMD | ~all US AMD | ❌ | +$1.3–13/kg Fe | Prohibitive | ❌ Disqualified |

**Recommended deployment:** copperas from one large sulfate-route TiO₂ plant (EU primary; China alternate), co-located for feed and power, target ~100 kt Fe/yr. Pair with an SPL co-location pilot in the US Rust Belt (Nucor / Steel Dynamics / Cleveland-Cliffs Great Lakes cluster, TRL 6–7, direct experimental proof) to de-risk the cell and start producing while the copperas offtake/regulatory work matures.

**Model updates implied by this memo** (for the techno-economic Monte Carlo, cf. `dark_mill_*.json` scenarios):
- Red-mud feedstock credit: **−$100 → −$20 to −$60/t Fe** (less attractive than modeled).
- Red-mud acid cost: **$2M/yr → $15–20M/yr** for 100 kt Fe (5–10× worse than modeled).
- SPL credit −$416.67/t Fe: **supported** (−$300 to −$600 range).
- New copperas scenario to add: feed cost **−$111 to −$278/t Fe, ~556 kt/yr copperas → 100 kt Fe/yr, single large sulfate TiO₂ plant.**
